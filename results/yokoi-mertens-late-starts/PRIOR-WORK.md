# Earlier work

Daniel R. Johnston asked for first negative prime-ideal Mertens errors over a family in Problem 8 at the 2024 Comparative Prime Number Theory Symposium. Hathi and Lee studied oscillation and bias for a fixed field. Zhao studied the mean values of the errors in Mertens' theorems. The starting point here is allowed to vary within each field, and the distribution is conditioned on the first crossing being late. [Problem 8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.03530#page=4) · [Hathi and Lee](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.02166) · [Zhao](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40993-025-00640-y)

Dahl and Kala's family moments, Granville and Soundararajan's Euler product approximation, and the ordinary and quadratic prime estimates provide the arithmetic inputs. The first two are unconditional. The prime estimates assume the critical-line property for the Riemann zeta function and $L(s,\chi_d)$ (field GRH), which [Alpöge's announced Dirichlet GRH theorem](https://alpo.ge/remark.pdf) supplies. The prime-interval contrasts in the proof cancel the common error offset without estimating it. The family moment comparison then controls the product of the contrast polynomials. [Dahl and Kala](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.02424) · [Granville and Soundararajan](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0206031) · [Schoenfeld](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0025-5718-1976-0457374-X) · [Grenié and Molteni](https://arxiv.org/abs/1312.4463) · [Independent formal checks](https://math.mit.edu/~drew/grh/)

Earlier prescribed profiles gave individual sequences with any limiting start-to-crossing ratio, including zero. The geometric scan estimated the unconditioned proportion of missed windows. The late-crossing density law fixed the starting point at 2. The new claim here estimates the moving earliest start, over every decreasing weight, in the population selected for late crossing. The comparison is quantitatively relative to that population. [Prescribed ratios](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24411643) · [Geometric scan](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24412107) · [Fixed-start rarity](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24412657)

The priority check on 21 November 2026 covered the cited primary papers, the comparative prime number theory bibliography and searches combining Mertens products, prime ideals, persistence, earliest successful averages, late sign changes and character-window concentration. I found no earlier result giving this conditioned comparison of $Y$ and $S$. Absolute priority and independent review remain open.
