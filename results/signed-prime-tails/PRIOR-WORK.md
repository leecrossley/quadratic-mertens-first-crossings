# Comparison with earlier work

Daniel R. Johnston posed Problem 8 during Ethan Lee’s talk at CPNTS in 2024. Its family question asks for a bound on the first sign change of the prime-ideal product error. A negative $y/t$ average is a sufficient condition for a negative raw error somewhere after the starting point. The two starting quantities can be very different. [Original problem, pp. 4–5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.03530#page=4)

Hathi and Lee treat fixed-field oscillations and bias for the prime-ideal product. Lamzouri established the rational product bias. Büthe obtained a first-sign-change bound for the ordinary reciprocal-prime sum and isolated its square contribution. Zhao treated quadratic reciprocal-prime sums, including the identity with the signed prime tail, and integrated their errors from 2 to $X$ against $dx$. The signed tail, prime-square bias and explicit-formula method have these precedents. [Hathi and Lee](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.02166) · [Lamzouri](https://arxiv.org/abs/1410.3777) · [Büthe, §§4–5](https://www.math.ubc.ca/~gerg/teaching/592-Fall2018/papers/2015.Buthe.pdf) · [Zhao, Theorem 3 and §6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.18903)

Fourier tests for sign changes in prime races go back to Ingham and Stark. Their uses, including computation in place of independence assumptions, are discussed by Ford and Konyagin. An earlier Yokoi result uses the factor $1-y/p$ on a geometric grid, with an exceptional set in its conductor estimate. [Ford and Konyagin, §3](https://personal.math.ubc.ca/~gerg/teaching/592-Fall2018/papers/2002.Ford_2.pdf) · [Earlier geometric-grid theorem and its exceptional set](https://github.com/leecrossley/yokoi-mertens-geometric-starts/blob/v1.0.0/PROOF.md)

The specific deductions here are the two-sided finite-window formula with a field-independent rational correction, and the individual bound in terms of the two local sums over quadratic zeros. The November 21, 2026 search covered these authors, quadratic Mertens averages, signed reciprocal-prime tails, single-frequency sign tests and first crossings in conductor ranges. No matching theorem was located in those sources. The result has not been independently peer reviewed.

## The nearest zero and central multiplicity

The earlier [two-point theorem](PROOF.md) bounded the log of a negative starting point using sums of inverse squared distances involving the centre and one chosen zero. Noncentral pairs with tiny separations could make that estimate arbitrarily large. The [nearest-zero theorem](NEAREST-ZERO.md) eliminates both sums from a first-start bound. The older theorem additionally supplied a signed margin on a prescribed late interval. The seven-frequency test, the necessary finite prime inequalities and the sign witness obtained after withholding all central ordinates are studied here for this specific $y/t$ average. [Prime inequalities](PRIME-TEST.md) · [Withheld-data certificate](WITHHELD.md)

The November 21, 2026 search covered quadratic Mertens averages, first crossings in the conductor aspect, low-lying Dirichlet zeros and Chebyshev bias, Fourier sign tests with central zeros or close pairs, and zero configurations that obstruct prime races. The comparison included all proof notes in this collection. The nearest known results address the following questions.

- Diamond and Pintz proved oscillations of the ordinary prime product. Hathi and Lee studied the number-field product and its bias. Büthe's numerical first-sign-change bound is for the ordinary Mertens reciprocal-prime sum. Zhao averages errors from 2 to a growing endpoint. [Diamond and Pintz](https://doi.org/10.5802/jtnb.687) · [Hathi and Lee](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.02166) · [Büthe](https://www.impan.pl/shop/publication/transaction/download/product/91021) · [Zhao](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.18903)
- Bays, Ford, Hudson and Rubinstein related small quadratic zeros to Chebyshev bias. Devin treated bias and logarithmic densities for general analytic functions. Carneiro, Chandee and Milinovich developed explicit-formula bounds for central orders and lowest zeros. These are precedents for using low zeros to study prime statistics and for the prime side of the present test. [Bays and coauthors](https://www.math.uwaterloo.ca/~mrubinst/publications/163.pdf) · [Devin](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.06394) · [Carneiro and coauthors](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.00955)
- Ford, Konyagin and Lamzouri constructed hypothetical off-line zero configurations for races between primes in residue classes. The [shifted lattices](GRID.md) here lie on the critical line and give an exact sign bound for both kernels of the future average. Fejér smoothing, positivity of Fourier moment matrices, and periodization of a lattice are classical ingredients. [Ford, Konyagin and Lamzouri](https://ford126.web.illinois.edu/wwwpapers/barriersIII.pdf)
- Dahl and Lamzouri discuss the relation between the Chowla and Yokoi discriminants in their work on class numbers. Daniel R. Johnston's Problem 8 on the 2024 list asks about the first sign change of the raw product. The family-wide question for this fixed weighted average is a related, more specific question. [Dahl and Lamzouri](https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.00889) · [Problem 8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.03530#page=4)

No matching result for this fixed future average was found in those sources. The priority search is bounded by the sources identified here. The analytic number theory and the application to all fields satisfying the stated criteria are written proofs. Four lemmas of the argument have Lean checks, and the individual-field inequalities have reproducible interval certificates. Independent peer review remains open.
