# Prior work and scope

Daniel R. Johnston posed Problem 8 during the 2024 Comparative Prime Number Theory Symposium. It asks for bounds on the first crossing of a number field's Mertens product. Here $`F_d`$ averages the logarithmic error at all future arguments using $`y\,dt/t^2`$. The earlier nearest-zero theorem supplies the implication from a late $`F_d`$ to a low quadratic zero. Earlier work on a specific Yokoi field supplies the comparison with 222 trillion. [Problem list](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.03530v1) · [Nearest-zero theorem](https://github.com/leecrossley/quadratic-mertens-first-crossings/blob/cae0113ded2504f0f29857045ca2a3028ddaab55/results/signed-prime-tails/NEAREST-ZERO.md) · [Single-field certificate](https://github.com/leecrossley/quadratic-mertens-first-crossings/blob/cae0113ded2504f0f29857045ca2a3028ddaab55/results/fixed-weight-delay-2380853/PROOF.md)

Stéphane Louboutin and Marc Munsch study real zeros of the theta function for real even primitive characters. They give the Gaussian transformation and a sufficient condition on partial sums for theta positivity. Arshay Sheth and Wayne Voo use positive theta functions to obtain positivity of derivatives at the centre. The signed-mass estimate here allows the theta function to change sign. [Louboutin and Munsch, equations (1)–(3) and Theorem 4](https://publi.math.unideb.hu/paper/1837/download/10_5486_PMD_2013_5670.pdf) · [Sheth and Voo](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.12152v1)

Oleksiy Klurman, Youness Lamzouri and Marc Munsch prove many sign changes in short character sums for typical fundamental discriminants. Their theorem is a distribution result over fields. Zikang Dong, Shengbo Zhao and Guang-Liang Zhou explicitly induce characters to moduli divisible by all small primes. Their argument in §2 uses a conjectured upper bound on character sums to deduce a zero-location statement. Removing small prime factors and using Mellin transforms to detect zeros both predate this work. [Klurman, Lamzouri and Munsch](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.02195) · [Dong, Zhao and Zhou, §2](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.11389v1)

The use of Mellin zeros to deduce sign changes has a longer history. Pribitkin gives oscillation results for coefficients of general Dirichlet series using Laguerre's rule of signs. Here the field and the deletion modulus both vary, and the signed witnesses have specified sizes and a polynomial deadline in the primitive conductor. [Pribitkin, Proceedings of the AMS (2008)](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9939-08-09296-4)

V. Chandee's critical-line upper bound gives the conductor dependence in the proof. D. Platt and T. Trudgian have already ruled out real zeros for the even quadratic characters in their finite conductor range, which includes the numerical example here. The first low zero in that example had also been bracketed in the earlier prime-average calculation. [Chandee, Corollary 1.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0906.4177#page=3) · [Platt and Trudgian](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29018) · [Earlier zero brackets](https://github.com/leecrossley/quadratic-mertens-first-crossings/blob/cae0113ded2504f0f29857045ca2a3028ddaab55/results/fixed-weight-delay-2380853/brackets.txt)

The claim made here is the individual-field implication from a long delay to an ordered deficit and recovery, with quantitative sizes, uniformly after any selected deletions through $`(\log d)^2`$ and with both witnesses by $`d^8`$. It also gives the polynomial localisation and signed-mass estimate for larger deletion moduli. The literature search covered quadratic theta positivity, real and low nonreal zeros, sign changes of short character sums, induced characters with small primes removed, and the maintained Mertens-crossing results. No earlier proof of these statements was found in the sources checked on 21 November 2026. This record is a preprint and has not undergone independent peer review.
