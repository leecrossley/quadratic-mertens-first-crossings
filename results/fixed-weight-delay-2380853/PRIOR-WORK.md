# Comparison with earlier work

Daniel R. Johnston asked for the first negative value of the Mertens product error in simple number fields and for a first-sign-change bound in a family. He posed the question during Ethan Lee’s talk in 2024. The fixed future average here is a stronger sign test associated with that question. [CPNTS Problem 8, pp. 4–5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.03530#page=4)

The field $d=2{,}380{,}853$ was already singled out in a census of 8,950 Yokoi fields. Its raw product crosses near 143,054.03953. If one may choose any decreasing weight, the infimum of successful starts is between 137,123.79815 and 137,123.79818. Those results use a choice of weight and do not determine the sign for the particular infinite $y/t$ weight used here. The previous density theorem allows exceptional fields, and the delayed-start construction gives other fields with provable delays. [Census and optimal-weight result](https://github.com/leecrossley/yokoi-mertens-optimal-starts/blob/v1.0.0/PROOF.md) · [Density theorem](https://github.com/leecrossley/yokoi-mertens-geometric-starts/blob/v1.0.0/PROOF.md) · [Delayed-start construction](https://github.com/leecrossley/yokoi-mertens-power-start-delays/blob/v1.0.0/PROOF.md)

Smoothing explicit formulas with certified zeros has precedents in Büthe’s first-sign-change calculation. Hathi and Lee study the raw prime-ideal Mertens error. Zhao’s quadratic estimates integrate twisted prime-sum errors from 2 to $X$ against $dx$. These are earlier sources for the methods and for the difference that a choice of averaging weight makes. [Büthe](https://www.math.ubc.ca/~gerg/teaching/592-Fall2018/papers/2015.Buthe.pdf) · [Hathi and Lee](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.02166) · [Zhao](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.18903)

The claim here is the continuum bound through $d^{9/4}$ and the signed witness at $d^{7/3}$ in this specific field. The search on November 21, 2026 included the discriminant, the earlier census, fixed-weight quadratic Mertens averages and explicit prime-race sign bounds. No earlier matching interval or fixed-weight witness was found in the sources checked. A single discriminant leaves open which exponent, if any, covers all sufficiently large Yokoi fields.
