## Further results

- [A prime average that stays positive past 222 trillion](results/fixed-weight-delay-2380853/)
- [A signed-prime test for a negative Mertens average](results/signed-prime-tails/)
- [How the nearest zero and central multiplicity affect the first negative start](results/signed-prime-tails/NEAREST-ZERO.md)
- Under RH and quadratic Dirichlet GRH, in every sufficiently large field an isolated near-central pair permits a negative average by a fixed power. Delays past every fixed power force either arbitrarily many zeros in shrinking central intervals or, along subsequences with bounded central counts, arithmetic progressions in the limiting zero heights up to finitely many exceptions. [Proof and four-window test](results/signed-prime-tails/NEAR-CENTRAL.md) · [Checks and replay](results/signed-prime-tails/README.md#near-central-zeros-on-the-conductor-scale)
- Under RH and quadratic Dirichlet GRH, in every sufficiently large field real measurements give a polynomial-window sign test that allows central crowding. Limiting progressions in a delaying sequence must have multiplicity-weighted density at least $1/(2\pi)$. A finite calculation proves three changes of sign in discriminant 2,380,853 from 51 real measurements. The family-wide exponent remains open. [Real-axis theorem](results/signed-prime-tails/REAL-AXIS.md) · [Split and inert prime sums](results/signed-prime-tails/REAL-AXIS-PRIMES.md) · [Four sign witnesses](results/signed-prime-tails/REAL-AXIS-FIELD.md) · [Checks and replay](results/signed-prime-tails/README.md#testing-the-prime-average-on-the-real-axis) · [51-value review packet](results/signed-prime-tails/review/README.md)
- Under RH and quadratic Dirichlet GRH, the averages in discriminants 5 and 13 are positive for every real start from 2. [Maintained proof](results/yokoi-mertens-positive-averages-5-13/PROOF.md) [Published proof](https://github.com/leecrossley/yokoi-mertens-positive-averages-5-13/blob/9a1e51b0e44717da1707423936b654b3bc4c3ac4/PROOF.md) [Corrected release](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24416721) [Original release](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24415535)
- A central zero or a sufficiently close pair constrains the scalar prime test in large quadratic fields. A separate prime-sign model gives obstructions to tests at several frequencies. [Hypotheses and proof](results/split-inert-prime-test-obstruction/) [Published statement](https://github.com/leecrossley/split-inert-prime-test-obstruction/blob/5bd0dd16db4bdb364939a701d2b56eab4b8234b8/README.md) [Release](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24416395)
- Under RH and quadratic Dirichlet GRH, the weighted disagreement with discriminant 5 rises and falls before a fixed power of each sufficiently large positive fundamental discriminant. The threshold for the discriminant is ineffective. A late average in a Yokoi field requires a finite adverse lead of order at least the square root of the log discriminant. [Maintained proof](results/quadratic-prime-disagreement-windows/PROOF.md) [Archived release](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24416496)
- With sufficiently large spacing, a model on ordinary primes adds two independently chosen zero patterns to its base functions and gives a large rise in the weighted lead. Its average stays positive from 2 onwards with a double central zero. For each fixed positive exponent, a sufficiently close pair gives positivity from 2 up to that power of the reference parameter. The construction assumes the critical-line property for the four fixed factors of conductors 1, 5, 13 and 65. [Maintained proof](results/quadratic-prime-disagreement-model/PROOF.md) [Archived release](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24416498)

- In many Yokoi fields the average is positive from 2 across a range longer than every fixed power of the log discriminant. At sufficiently large starts in this range the untwisted bias exceeds every quadratic correlation in the stated conductor range. The proof assumes RH and quadratic Dirichlet GRH. [Statement and checks](results/yokoi-delay-small-character-correlations/) [Family note](results/yokoi-delay-small-character-correlations/FAMILY-NOTE.md) [Archived releases](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24417504)
- In an ordinary-prime model the untwisted bias can grow while every fixed secondary quadratic correlation stays bounded through a prescribed horizon. The proof uses RH and quadratic Dirichlet GRH. [Statement and checks](results/quadratic-twist-correlation-obstruction/) [Family note](results/quadratic-twist-correlation-obstruction/FAMILY-NOTE.md) [Archived releases](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24417499)
- A long delay forces a signed count on integers free of small prime factors to go negative and then recover. Both witnesses occur by $`d^8`$ for any selection of primes through the squared log discriminant, including a field with a central zero. The proof uses RH and quadratic Dirichlet GRH. [Proof, replay and evidence](results/rough-character-deficits/)
  Removing every eligible inert prime gives the latest first negative. In $`d=2{,}380{,}853`$ it occurs at 49,937, and all $`2^{47}`$ selections also recover by $`d^6`$ unconditionally. Retaining 43 delays the first recovery to 137,705,720,539. [Sieve choice and certificates](results/rough-character-deficits/SIEVE-CHOICE.md)
