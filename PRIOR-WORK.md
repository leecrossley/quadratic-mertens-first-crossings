# Literature and scope

Daniel R. Johnston posed Problem 8 during Ethan Lee's talk at the 2024 Comparative Prime Number Theory Symposium. Alia Hamieh, Habiba Kadiri, Greg Martin and Nathan Ng recorded it in their problem list. It asks when the prime-ideal Mertens product first drops below its main term and asks for a bound for a family of fields. The quantity compared here is the same product with the same threshold. [Problem 8, pp. 4–5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.03530#page=4)

Shehzad Hathi and Ethan Lee studied sign changes and limiting distributions for a fixed number field. The earlier Yokoi note gave a density-one upper bound, a lower bound for the greatest delay, and finite computations. The new question is how fast that greatest delay grows with a discriminant limit $D$. A late crossing requires positive error throughout the entire preceding interval. An unusually large error at one endpoint allows the product to have crossed earlier. [Hathi and Lee, Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.02166#page=3) · [Earlier family result](https://github.com/leecrossley/yokoi-mertens-crossing-bounds/releases/tag/v1.0.0)

**Existing tools.** Alexander Dahl and Vítězslav Kala model the splitting of rational primes in this family. Their Proposition 14 gives the quantitative comparison for a character evaluated at an integer. Andrew Granville and K. Soundararajan approximate quadratic $L$-values by Euler products and count the exceptional characters in their Proposition 2.2. Soundararajan developed the resonance method for extreme values of zeta and $L$-functions. Schoenfeld's explicit Chebyshev estimates give the ordinary prime-product error on the critical-line theorem for zeta. The dyadic fourth-moment argument is an elementary maximal inequality. [Dahl and Kala, Proposition 14](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1611.02424#page=11) · [Granville and Soundararajan, Proposition 2.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0206031#page=7) · [Soundararajan on resonance](https://arxiv.org/abs/0708.3990) · [Schoenfeld, Theorem 10](https://doi.org/10.2307/2005976)

**Contribution.** The weight here gives primes above a fixed threshold a negative bias proportional to $(\log p)^2/\sqrt p$, while restricting the length of the character polynomial used to transfer the weight to actual fields. A fourth-moment bound controls all prefixes of every dyadic interval. Together these steps produce fields with $S_d>\exp(c(\log D)^{1/5})$ for every sufficiently large $D$. Earlier results on small values of $L(1,\chi_d)$ compare a single Euler product with a limiting distribution. They supply inputs to the proof but do not by themselves control the entire path to the first crossing.
