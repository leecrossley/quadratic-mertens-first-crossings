# Literature and scope

Johnston posed the 2024 first-crossing question for the prime-ideal Mertens product. Hathi and Lee studied fixed-field oscillations and distributions. Zhao studied mean values of other Mertens errors. An earlier family note showed that fields crossing before any prescribed diverging deadline have density one, using weak convergence on finitely many windows and a zero–one law. That proof supplies no rate in the deadline and does not require the same start to work for a local and an infinite weighted integral. [Problem 8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.03530#page=4) · [Hathi and Lee](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.02166) · [Zhao](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.18903) · [Earlier density theorem, §3](https://github.com/leecrossley/yokoi-mertens-crossing-bounds/blob/v1.0.0/PROOF.md)

The present contribution is the power-saving bound on the proportion missed by a predetermined geometric scan and simultaneous success of two decreasing averages on a positive fraction of its windows. The analytic inputs are Cramér's ordinary mean estimate as applied by Diamond and Pintz, the short Euler product of Granville and Soundararajan, and Dahl and Kala's moment comparison. Only Cramér's estimate is conditional, on RH for the Riemann zeta function. Concentration of bounded independent sums is classical. [Diamond and Pintz, (4.3)](https://www.numdam.org/item/10.5802/jtnb.687.pdf#page=7) · [Granville and Soundararajan](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0206031#page=7) · [Dahl and Kala](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1611.02424#page=11) · [Hoeffding](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1963.10500830)

The literature check on 21 November 2026 included the primary papers, the comparative prime number theory bibliography, the public citation neighbourhood of the problem list and the two number-field papers, and searches on geometric starts, persistence of partial Euler products, weighted Mertens errors and first crossings in real quadratic families. I found no earlier estimate with this scan or exceptional-set rate. This search cannot establish absolute priority.
